# The 8 Experiences — full site copy

Each card has two sides. **Inside** = first-person, in two voices 800 years apart: Angelo DiLullo (Simply Always Awake; *Awake: It's Your Turn*, 2021) and **Dōgen Zenji** (1200–1253, *Shōbōgenzō*, *Fukanzazengi*) — "the modern teacher" and "the ancestor." **Behind the scenes** = the neuroscience. Quotes are verbatim unless marked [paraphrase] or [secondhand]; every Dōgen quote carries its translator (wording varies enormously by translation).

---

## Card 1 — No one home: the end of the narrator

**Inside.** The running commentary — the "me" that remembers, plans, rehearses, and narrates — goes silent. Not suppressed: simply absent. Angelo: *"You'll never get anyone who's had no-self realization to tell you what that self was that went away — because it's not anything."* Preferences remain, choices still get made, but the self woven through them is gone: *"Preferences are there, but the seeming self woven into all of it is what's gone… Choices are made — but if I'm really accurate, it's almost like the whole environment just makes the choice. It's a self-propagating, self-responsive environment."* After liberation, he says, *"identity is essentially dissolved into presence."*

**Behind the scenes.** The brain's "narrator" lives largely in the **default mode network (DMN)** — especially the posterior cingulate cortex (PCC) and medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), which run self-referential thought, autobiographical memory, and mental time travel. Judson Brewer's lab (2011) showed the DMN deactivates in experienced meditators, with the PCC quieting specifically in the deepest states: less DMN activity, less "someone" having the experience. A 2025 intensively-sampled 7T fMRI case study of an adept moving through Theravada insight stages found self-related regions (mPFC, temporal poles) consistently deactivating while perceptual regions activated — and equanimity correlated with reduced mPFC/PCC activity (Yang et al., 2025). In predictive-processing terms, the DMN sits at the top of the brain's predictive hierarchy; "ego dissolution" is what it feels like when high-level priors — including the prior that *I am a particular person with a past and future* — relax their grip (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019, REBUS). Philosophers Letheby & Gerrans (2017) frame it as the unbinding of the brain's integrative "self-binding" processes — and note the self-model is layered: narrative self (DMN) can dissolve while embodied self persists, or vice versa.

**Try it.** Self-inquiry: sit quietly and *wait for the next thought* — watch so precisely that you catch it arising. Ask: "For whom does this thought arise?" Notice the gap between the assumed thinker and the thought itself. (Angelo's own method.)

**The ancestor (Dōgen, 1200–1253).** *Genjōkōan* (trans. Tanahashi): *"To study the buddha way is to study the self. To study the self is to forget the self. To forget the self is to be actualized by myriad things. When actualized by myriad things, your body and mind as well as the bodies and minds of others drop away."* And from the *Fukanzazengi* (trans. Bielefeldt): *"Body and mind of themselves will drop away, and your original face will be manifest."* Eight hundred years before the DMN was named, the same report: the self is something that can be *forgotten*, and what remains is not absence but actualization.

## Card 2 — The wall comes down: inner/outer non-duality

**Inside.** The felt division between "me in here" and "world out there" collapses — not as an idea but as perception. Angelo on non-dual perception: *"The thing about non-dual perception is it really does look different. The sense of distance and space can be almost non-existent; the sense of intimacy is much more pronounced… You can still see how the mind tries to put things together in a subject-object way, but there's no identity taking itself from that. So it's just a thought. It's like watching a picture on a screen."* And: *"Me as that cup, me as that keyboard, me as that book — when identity drops away, you see that it actually was an illusion… You literally experience all of that as totally non-separate. And there's not an awareness apart from an experiencing. It's that close. It's that intimate."* His technical term for what stops: **subjectification** — *"the movement in consciousness to create a subject and an object."* When it stops: *"you could call that unbound consciousness."*

**Behind the scenes.** The brain normally runs two large-scale systems in opposition: an **intrinsic network** (self-related, inward) and an **extrinsic network** (task/externally oriented), which are *anticorrelated* — when one is active, the other quiets. Zoran Josipovic (NYU) put Tibetan Buddhist meditators in fMRI and found that during **non-dual-awareness meditation specifically**, this anticorrelation breaks down: both systems stay active simultaneously (Josipovic et al., 2012). The brain stops toggling between "in here" and "out there" — phenomenologically, exactly non-duality. In his 2024 update, Josipovic proposes the **central precuneus network** (central precuneus coupled with dlPFC, dACC, dorsomedial PFC, inferior parietal lobule) as the integrative neural correlate of non-dual awareness: uniquely positioned to couple with *both* intrinsic and extrinsic systems. Meanwhile the **salience network** (anterior insula, dACC) does the opposite — it monitors and *increases* intrinsic/extrinsic segregation (Josipovic, 2024).

**Try it.** Sound as gateway: walking, washing dishes, anywhere — *"put your attention fully into sound with 100% of your attention, not differentiating, just sound as sound."* Notice how sound *"is completely all-encompassing and leaves no room for separation."*

**The ancestor (Dōgen, 1200–1253).** *Genjōkōan* (trans. Tanahashi): *"To carry the self forward and illuminate myriad things is delusion. That myriad things come forth and illuminate the self is awakening."* The direction of illumination reverses: not the self reaching out to the world, but the world arriving as the self. And: *"When you see forms or hear sounds, fully engaging body-and-mind, you intuit dharma intimately"* — intimacy as the signature of non-separation, exactly Angelo's "that close, that intimate."

**Sources:** Josipovic et al. 2012, Front. Hum. Neurosci. · Josipovic 2024, Front. Psychol. · BatGap transcript; ZDoggMD "Awakening Explained Ep. 2" (Angelo quotes)

## Card 3 — No edges: spacelessness and boundlessness

**Inside.** Space itself — the felt sense of being *located* somewhere, with the world at a distance — thins or vanishes. What remains is boundless and edgeless. Angelo describes the ground beneath experience as *"the undifferentiated or the unconditioned — undifferentiated aliveness, not blankness or bare awareness. It's more like the substance of substancelessness from which substance arises."* [Secondhand transcription — treat as near-verbatim:] *"There can be realized this experience of a sort of formless, aware space — but it's not so much a space, and it feels as if everything sort of appears out of it and disappears back into it… to hold awareness as some kind of final state, stage, background, or ground of experience — you're holding on to subtle conceptualities without realizing it."* His warning matters: even "boundless awareness" can become a subtle concept to cling to.

**Behind the scenes.** Your sense of *where you are* is constructed, mainly by parietal cortex. Newberg's SPECT imaging of Tibetan Buddhist meditators in deep meditation showed decreased activity in the superior parietal lobule — the "orientation association area" that maps the body's position in space — with the decrease inversely related to frontal attention increases; his interpretation: blocking input to this area accompanies *"the sense of no space and no time"* (Newberg et al., 2001). The **temporoparietal junction (TPJ)** specifically encodes self-location: stimulating the right angular gyrus electrically *caused* out-of-body experiences (Blanke et al., 2002, Nature); conflicting visual-tactile input in VR relocated participants' felt self outside their bodies (Lenggenhager et al., 2007, Science); lesion studies confirm TPJ damage produces pathological self-location errors (Ionta et al., 2011, Neuron). Most precisely: a MEG study of an expert meditator (20,000+ hours) producing graded self-boundary dissolution found **right-lateralized beta oscillations in TPJ plus medial parietal cortex tracked the dissolution grade** — and at the deepest grade, the phenomenological category of *time* vanished along with the boundary: *"time is a mirror reflection of the self-boundary"* (Dor-Ziderman et al., 2016). A preregistered 2024 replication (N=46) confirmed high-beta (21–30 Hz) suppression in frontoparietal and posterior medial cortices in "full dissolvers" reporting suspended agency and dissolved first-person perspective (Trautwein et al., 2024).

**Try it.** Sit and notice the felt boundary of "you" — usually drawn at the skin. Ask: where, exactly, does hearing end and the hearer begin? Let the question stay open rather than answering it.

**The ancestor (Dōgen, 1200–1253).** *Genjōkōan* (trans. Tanahashi): *"Enlightenment is like the moon reflected on the water. The moon does not get wet, nor is the water broken… Enlightenment does not divide you, just as the moon does not break the water… Each reflection, however long or short its duration, manifests the vastness of the dewdrop, and realizes the limitlessness of the moonlight in the sky."* (Traditional reading: moon = enlightenment, water = the person — a non-duality teaching.) And: *"When you find your place where you are, practice occurs, actualizing the fundamental point… for the place, the way, is neither large nor small, neither yours nor others."*

## Card 4 — The eternal now: timelessness

**Inside.** Past and future drop out — not forgotten, but revealed as constructions. What remains is a vivid, edgeless present. From *Awake*: *"Awakening is not an event. It's not even a thing. There is only life, and life knows no boundary, no separation, no time."* His inquiry pointer: *"What are you when you don't think about yourself, or your past, or your future?… You find yourself in a space with no past and no future… The sounds haven't gone anywhere, have they? The colors and shapes in front of you are still there. In fact, they are a bit more vivid, aren't they?… Yet there is no story, no past, no future, no 'substance' of what you always thought of as you."* He offers a striking pointer from physics (flagged as experiential, not scientific): *"For a photon, a massless photon, it experiences time as eternity, right?… There's also no distance… Right, but we believe our thoughts over proven science."*

**Behind the scenes.** Subjective time is built, not given. The **insula** translates body signals into felt duration (Craig's model): climbing activation in posterior insula tracks stimulus length, and interoceptive focus amplifies time distortion (Pollatos et al., 2014). The division of labor: cerebellum stabilizes sub-second timing, basal ganglia accumulate evidence over longer durations via dopamine dynamics, cortex integrates temporal priors (2026 review, Hajnal et al.). In active-inference models, ego dissolution is a collapse in the **"temporal thickness"** of the self-model — the capacity to model consequences over time — via lowered precision on high-level priors (Deane, 2020). Empirically: 22 highly experienced meditators showed meditation made body boundaries less salient, reduced attention to time, and sped felt time passage — while basic metronome timing was *unchanged*, showing the effect is on higher-order time *experience*, not the inner clock (Linares Gutiérrez et al., 2022). Metzinger's philosophical framing: *"temporal self-location"* means being present in an experienced "now" — and *"'Nowness' itself is a form of temporal experience,"* so in minimal phenomenal experience, time representation itself is transiently suspended (Metzinger, 2020). His survey work (N=1,403) factor-analyzed pure-consciousness reports into 12 factors including "Time, Effort and Desire" and "Emptiness and Non-egoic Self-awareness" (Gamma & Metzinger, 2021).

**Try it.** Close your eyes for 60 seconds and locate "the past." Not memories *of* the past — the past itself, as a present-moment experience. Notice what's actually there: thoughts *about* then, arising now.

**The ancestor (Dōgen, 1200–1253).** *Uji* — "The Time-Being" (trans. Welch & Tanahashi): *"'For the time being' here means time itself is being, and all being is time."* And: *"The way the self arrays itself is the form of the entire world. See each thing in this entire world as a moment of time… This is the understanding that the self is time."* Dōgen doesn't place you *in* time; time is what you are. *Genjōkōan* (Tanahashi): *"Firewood becomes ash, and it does not become firewood again. Yet, do not suppose that the ash is after and the firewood before… Birth is an expression complete this moment. Death is an expression complete this moment."*

**Related:** No edges · No one home
**Sources:** Pollatos et al. 2014, PLoS ONE · Deane 2020, Philos. Mind Sci. · Metzinger 2020 · Gamma & Metzinger 2021, PLoS ONE · Linares Gutiérrez et al. 2022, Biology · Hajnal et al. 2026, The Cerebellum · Goodreads quotes; ZDoggMD Ep. 2 (Angelo quotes)

## Card 5 — More real than real: vividness and clarity

**Inside.** Paradoxically, as the self thins, the world gets *clearer* — colors, sounds, textures arrive with startling immediacy. Angelo calls *"more real than real"* a litmus test for genuine recognition: *"It doesn't need a litmus test once this really clarifies."* Of his own awakening night: *"Literally everything was pure conscious being… It was so much more real than real. It was self-obvious and self-validating."* What replaced the world of thought-struggle, he writes, *"was boundless clarity and intimacy with all immediate textures of phenomena."*

**Behind the scenes.** Predictive-processing models explain the paradox elegantly: perception is a negotiation between top-down predictions (priors) and bottom-up sensory data. **Relax high-level priors and sensory data floods through with higher precision** — the world literally looks brighter because the brain stops painting over it with expectations (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019, REBUS). Empirical support: expert meditators in Open Presence (non-dual) vs. focused attention during an auditory oddball task showed a pattern of *"increased sensory monitoring and reduced perceptual inferences"* — the neural signature of relaxed prior-formation (Fucci et al., 2018). A 2025 study found meditators don't have sharper senses — they have a **lower decision criterion**: lower prestimulus alpha in somatosensory cortex, "keeping the gate more open," relying less on prior expectations and more on incoming data (Mylius et al., 2025). Laukkonen & Slagter (2021) place practices on a continuum: focused attention → open monitoring → non-dual meditation, each relinquishing increasingly engrained habits of prediction, *"until all conceptual processing falls away, unveiling a state of pure awareness."* A 2025 7T fMRI study of insight stages concluded advanced meditation reduces the *"stickiness" of prior predictions*, letting processing happen at earlier, rawer levels of the hierarchy (Yang et al., 2025).

**Try it.** Look at any object for 30 seconds. First notice the *labels* the mind pastes on ("cup, white, mine"). Then drop the labels and meet the raw visual texture. The vividness jump is the priors relaxing — a small taste.

**The ancestor (Dōgen, 1200–1253).** *Fukanzazengi* (Cleary-based rendering): *"It is the presence of things as they are."* And his instruction for non-thinking — *hishiryō* (trans. Waddell & Abe): *"Think of not-thinking. How do you think of not-thinking? Nonthinking. This in itself is the essential art of zazen."* Scholars gloss *hishiryō* as a prereflective, non-conceptual vividness — "pure presence of things as they are" — not blankness. The priors drop; the suchness stays.

**Related:** The wall comes down · The ground of being
**Sources:** Carhart-Harris & Friston 2019 (REBUS) · Fucci et al. 2018, Neuropsychologia · Mylius et al. 2025, Psychophysiology · Laukkonen & Slagter 2021, Neurosci. Biobehav. Rev. · Yang et al. 2025, NeuroImage · BatGap transcript (Angelo quotes)

## Card 6 — Effortless being: the end of strain

**Inside.** The background effort of maintaining a self — managing, controlling, becoming — simply stops. Angelo: *"Life knows no separation. There is no strain. It flows effortlessly into existence, and dissolves without a trace just as effortlessly."* His core pointer is a stopping: *"Just stop, stop. Stop faking it. Stop imagining enlightenment. Stop thinking about the future. Stop trying to do anything. Just stop, stop, stop… It's really a very simple thing. It's a stopping… an act of pure being."* Post-awakening, *"non-doership is obvious"* — choices happen, but no one is pushing the river: *"it's almost like the whole environment just makes the choice."*

**Behind the scenes.** Real-time fMRI neurofeedback linked **PCC deactivation** to meditators' reports of *"effortless awareness"* (Garrison et al., 2013) — with an important caveat the authors themselves flag: demand characteristics (one participant revised her report to match the feedback graph), so treat as suggestive, not proven. Phenomenological studies of boundary dissolution describe **passive agency** — "non-doing," formless attention arising through "letting go" — as a core category (Nave et al., via Atad et al., 2025). At the neural level, meditation also involves *frontal control over early sensory processing*: experienced meditators showed a meditation-specific frontal negativity just 160 ms post-stimulus, source-localized to mPFC/PCC — the brain learning to override *"phylogenetically evolved perceptual and attentional processing automatisms"* (Kornmeier et al., 2019). Effortlessness, on this view, isn't passivity — it's the automatisms of selfing no longer firing.

**Try it.** The Stop pointer: wherever you are, *"stop thinking, stop making anything, stop trying to get somewhere, stop trying to become something, stop trying to avoid something. Stop."* Don't make stopping into a technique — just stop, once, completely.

**The ancestor (Dōgen, 1200–1253).** *Fukanzazengi*: *"Do not think good or bad. Do not administer pros and cons. Cease all the movements of the conscious mind, the gauging of all thoughts and views. Have no designs on becoming a Buddha."* And (trans. Bielefeldt): *"The Way is originally perfect and all-pervading. How could it be contingent on practice and realization? The true vehicle is self-sufficient. What need is there for special effort?"* Effortlessness as doctrine: there's nothing to become, so there's nothing to strain toward.

**Related:** No one home · The click
**Sources:** Garrison et al. 2013, Front. Hum. Neurosci. (with caveats) · Nave et al. 2021 / Atad et al. 2025 · Kornmeier et al. 2019, PLoS ONE · ZDoggMD Ep. 2; Bookey quote index (Angelo quotes)

## Card 7 — The ground of being: undifferentiated aliveness

**Inside.** When self, time, and space fall away, what remains isn't blankness — it's a vivid, aware aliveness without edges or center. Angelo's names for it: **"pristine, undivided nature,"** *"unbound consciousness,"* *"undifferentiated aliveness, not blankness or bare awareness."* From the book: *"It is when something vast and eternal steps forward into your conscious experience and recognizes itself in a very direct way."* His two-night awakening: night one, *"literally everything was pure conscious being… identity dissolved into it. There was no personal identity, there was a universal consciousness, an identity of pure being"*; the next day, *"the whole paradigm of identity and everything that has to do with identity just completely disappeared, and it never came back… There's just nothing there. But it's also not empty… this is home."*

**Behind the scenes.** What could "pure awareness" be, neurally? The 2025 **COGITATE** adversarial collaboration (N=256, preregistered, testing integrated information theory vs. global workspace theory) found sustained **posterior cortical integration** — a temporo-parieto-occipital "hot zone" — decoded conscious contents at >95% accuracy across the full stimulus duration, while prefrontal "broadcast" reached only ~70% and was temporally restricted — favoring posterior integration as the basis of *"what it is like"* (COGITATE Consortium, 2025, Nature). Josipovic (2024) bridges this to non-duality: NDA as *"the broadcast of the system's current ground state… the broadcast of its own broadcasting capacity."* EEG work on "pure awareness" in TM practitioners found a double dissociation: the state is marked by **moment-to-moment signal unpredictability and slow coordinated distant-region activity** — temporal entropy and aperiodic dynamics — while classic alpha phase-coherence contributed least; and it's a *state*, not a trait, with little carryover into rest (Canales-Johnson et al., 2026). During boundary dissolution, broadband **Lempel-Ziv complexity rises** — neural de-differentiation mirroring the psychedelic pattern (Atad et al., 2025). Metzinger's synthesis of 500+ pure-consciousness reports across 57 countries argues "selfiness" and "perspectivalness" are *contents* of consciousness, not consciousness itself (Metzinger, 2024, *The Elephant and the Blind*).

**Try it.** Notice awareness itself — not what you're aware *of*, but the aware space in which sounds, sensations, and thoughts appear. Angelo: *"What is this that is aware before, during, and after a thought?"* Rest the question rather than answering it.

**The ancestor (Dōgen, 1200–1253).** *Fukanzazengi* (trans. Bielefeldt): *"The Way is originally perfect and all-pervading… Indeed, the whole body is free from dust… your original face will be manifest."* "Original face" — the ground prior to parents, personality, ego: behind everything and everything at the same time. *Genjōkōan* (Tanahashi): *"No trace of realization remains, and this no-trace continues endlessly."*

## Card 8 — The click: recognition itself

**Inside.** Awakening, in Angelo's telling, is not an achievement but a **recognition** — sudden, obvious, and self-validating. His own moment: *"Oh, this is what spiritual people are looking for."* The click he describes: *"Oh, the container in which I've always been, is the being and the being is the thinking and the thinking is the thought, and everything I've ever thought about my life and the world is all the same stuff. It's pure unbound awakeness."* He insists it can't be forced — *"you can't control when an awakening happens"* — but the ground can be prepared: *"every time you do that, like the tumblers are falling. And you can't control when an awakening happens. You can't decide it's going to happen now or later or whatever, but you can keep coming back to that place."* What drives it isn't technique but yearning: *"What drives the inquiry is the most important thing… your desire to wake up, your desperation with the suffering of life, your deep, deep yearning to know what's what."*

**Behind the scenes.** [Honest framing: no study has captured the "click" of awakening itself — it is sudden, rare, and unplannable, the worst possible laboratory target.] The closest science is about **insight**: Laukkonen & Slagter (2021) attribute meditative insight experiences to **Bayesian model reduction** — the brain pruning redundant parameters from its generative model, experienced as sudden clarity. Deepening non-dual states show rising **gamma-band activity in ACC, precuneus, and superior parietal lobule** — integration hubs getting *more* active, not quieter, as non-duality deepens (Brown et al., 2018). And researchers can now **decode time-varying meditative depth** from EEG (theta/alpha/gamma connectivity), though conventional DMN regions of interest "fell short" of capturing the dynamics (Reggente et al., 2025). The site should present this card as the frontier: the phenomenology is precise, the neuroscience is still catching up.

**Try it.** Nothing to try — that's the point. But you can orient: *"Your will to awaken is what matters… orient to that part of yourself that knows there's a more real, authentic, undivided way to experience reality. And trust it."*

**The ancestor (Dōgen, 1200–1253) — and the honest tension.** Dōgen's Sōtō framework deliberately *dissolves* the sudden breakthrough into ongoing practice-realization, so there is no exact "click" equivalent. *Genjōkōan* (trans. Tanahashi): *"When buddhas are truly buddhas, they do not necessarily notice that they are buddhas. However, they are actualized buddhas, who go on actualizing buddha."* Where Angelo describes a click — *"like the tumblers are falling"* — Dōgen dissolves the before-and-after entirely: practice *is* realization. The site should surface this contrast, not smooth it over: the recognition isn't an event you wait for; it's what sitting already is.
