# Dōgen Zenji — the ancestor voice

## Who he is (for the site's teacher panel)

**Eihei Dōgen Zenji (1200–1253)** — Japanese Zen master, founder of the Sōtō school of Zen in Japan and of Eihei-ji monastery. He traveled to China (1223–1227), studied under the Caodong master Rujing, and awakened when Rujing declared **"body and mind dropped off"** (*shinjin datsuraku*) — a phrase that became the axis of his teaching. His major work is the **Shōbōgenzō** (95 fascicles); he also wrote the **Fukanzazengi** ("Universally Recommended Instructions for Zazen").

**His central doctrine: practice and realization are one** (*shushō*). Zazen is not a means to a later awakening; sitting *is* enlightenment actualizing itself. As the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy puts it, with practice-realization Dōgen "closes a dualistic gap, for example, between potentiality and actuality, between before and after."

**Why his voice matters here:** a 13th-century master describing, in dense phenomenological prose, exactly the territory the site maps — self forgotten, inner/outer dissolved, time as being, mountains as sutras. He is the deep-time anchor: Angelo describes the same territory from inside a modern life; Dōgen described it 800 years ago from inside a monastery.

**Editorial honesty (do not smooth this over):** Dōgen does not write first-person experience reports the way Angelo does — his passages are philosophical pointers. And he deliberately *dissolves* the sudden "click" of awakening into ongoing practice-realization. That tension with Angelo's "tumblers falling" is a feature of the site, not a bug — surface it on Card 8.

## Key fascicles (for the site's "further reading" links)

- **Genjōkōan** ("Actualizing the Fundamental Point") — the most quoted fascicle: forgetting the self, firewood and ash, the moon in water.
- **Uji** ("The Time-Being" / "Being-Time") — time itself is being; all being is time.
- **Sansuikyō** ("Mountains and Waters Sutra") — mountains and waters as the sutra itself; non-duality of practitioner and world.
- **Fukanzazengi** — zazen instructions: dropping off body and mind, non-thinking (*hishiryō*), no designs on becoming a Buddha.
- **Bendōwa** — practice-realization (*shushō ittō*) laid out as doctrine.

## Quote bank by experience card

### Card 1 — No one home
- *Genjōkōan* (Tanahashi): "To study the buddha way is to study the self. To study the self is to forget the self. To forget the self is to be actualized by myriad things. When actualized by myriad things, your body and mind as well as the bodies and minds of others drop away. No trace of realization remains, and this no-trace continues endlessly."
- *Fukanzazengi* (Bielefeldt): "Body and mind of themselves will drop away, and your original face will be manifest."

### Card 2 — The wall comes down
- *Genjōkōan* (Tanahashi): "To carry the self forward and illuminate myriad things is delusion. That myriad things come forth and illuminate the self is awakening."
- *Genjōkōan* (Tanahashi): "When you see forms or hear sounds, fully engaging body-and-mind, you intuit dharma intimately."

### Card 3 — No edges
- *Genjōkōan* (Tanahashi, *Treasury*, p. 31): "Enlightenment is like the moon reflected on the water. The moon does not get wet, nor is the water broken… Enlightenment does not divide you, just as the moon does not break the water… Each reflection, however long or short its duration, manifests the vastness of the dewdrop, and realizes the limitlessness of the moonlight in the sky." (Zen commentary: moon = enlightenment, water = the person — a non-duality teaching.)
- *Genjōkōan* (Tanahashi): "When you find your place where you are, practice occurs, actualizing the fundamental point… for the place, the way, is neither large nor small, neither yours nor others."

### Card 4 — The eternal now
- *Uji* (Welch & Tanahashi): "'For the time being' here means time itself is being, and all being is time."
- *Uji* (Welch & Tanahashi): "The way the self arrays itself is the form of the entire world. See each thing in this entire world as a moment of time… Thus the self setting itself out in array sees itself. This is the understanding that the self is time."
- *Genjōkōan* (Tanahashi): "Firewood becomes ash, and it does not become firewood again. Yet, do not suppose that the ash is after and the firewood before… Birth is an expression complete this moment. Death is an expression complete this moment. They are like winter and spring. You do not call winter the beginning of spring, nor summer the end of spring."

### Card 5 — More real than real
- *Fukanzazengi* (Cleary-based, via Wikipedia — flag as widely-repeated rendering): "It is the presence of things as they are."
- *Fukanzazengi* (Waddell & Abe): "Think of not-thinking. How do you think of not-thinking? Nonthinking. This in itself is the essential art of zazen." (Scholar gloss: *hishiryō* is a prereflective, non-conceptual vividness — not blankness.)

### Card 6 — Effortless being
- *Fukanzazengi* (via Wikipedia's Dōgen article): "Do not think good or bad. Do not administer pros and cons. Cease all the movements of the conscious mind, the gauging of all thoughts and views. Have no designs on becoming a Buddha."
- *Fukanzazengi* (Bielefeldt): "The Way is originally perfect and all-pervading. How could it be contingent on practice and realization? The true vehicle is self-sufficient. What need is there for special effort?"

### Card 7 — The ground of being
- *Fukanzazengi* (Bielefeldt): "The Way is originally perfect and all-pervading… Indeed, the whole body is free from dust." / "…your original face will be manifest." ("Original face": the ground prior to parents, personality, ego — behind everything and everything at the same time.)
- *Genjōkōan* (Tanahashi): "No trace of realization remains, and this no-trace continues endlessly."

### Card 8 — The click (the honest tension)
- *Genjōkōan* (Tanahashi): "When buddhas are truly buddhas, they do not necessarily notice that they are buddhas. However, they are actualized buddhas, who go on actualizing buddha."
- Suggested site framing: "Where Angelo describes a 'click' of recognition — 'like the tumblers are falling' — Dōgen dissolves the before-and-after entirely: practice *is* realization. The recognition isn't an event you wait for; it's what sitting already is."

## Design suggestion for the site

Give the Inside of each card **two voice tabs or a toggle**: "The modern teacher" (Angelo) / "The ancestor" (Dōgen). Same experience, 800 years apart. The juxtaposition is the argument: this territory is stable across centuries, cultures, and vocabularies.
