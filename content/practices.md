# Practices — try-it pointers for the site

Each practice is 3–5 minutes, needs no equipment, and can be done anywhere. Format per practice: the instruction (Angelo's voice), **what to notice** (first-person), and **what may be happening neurally** (science, labeled as speculative where it is). The site should include Angelo's caveat prominently: inquiry is driven by yearning, not technique — *"Inquiry is not a technique, because you're not doing something… What drives the inquiry is the most important thing, and what drives it is your desire to wake up."* And: *"Don't try to recreate your awakening."*

## 1. The Stop pointer

**Do:** Wherever you are, *"stop thinking, stop making anything, stop trying to get somewhere, stop trying to become something, stop trying to avoid something. Stop."* Not a technique — a single, complete stopping. *"It's really a very simple thing. It's a stopping… an act of pure being."*

**What to notice:** What remains when the project of becoming pauses? Most people report a sudden, vivid availability of the present moment — sounds a bit brighter, the body more present. That's the signal.

**What may be happening neurally:** Stopping the goal-directed control loop may disengage the task-positive networks and the DMN's future-oriented simulation simultaneously — a small, deliberate taste of the anticorrelation-breakdown seen in non-dual meditation (Josipovic et al., 2012). (Speculative — no study has measured the Stop pointer specifically.)

## 2. Sound as gateway

**Do:** Walking, washing dishes, anywhere: *"put your attention fully into sound with 100% of your attention, not differentiating, just sound as sound."*

**What to notice:** Sound is *"completely all-encompassing and leaves no room for separation."* Notice that you can't find a boundary between the hearing and the heard — the "hearer" is a label pasted on after the fact.

**What may be happening neurally:** Sustained non-discriminative attention to a sensory stream may bias processing toward bottom-up sensory data over top-down priors — the "open gate" pattern (lower prestimulus alpha, lower decision criterion) seen in expert meditators (Mylius et al., 2025). (Speculative — a plausible extrapolation.)

## 3. Self-inquiry: what is aware?

**Do:** Angelo's core question: *"What is this that is aware before, during, and after a thought?"* Sit and literally wait for the next thought — orient so precisely that you catch it arising. Then ask: *"For whom does this thought arise?"* Don't answer — rest the question.

**What may be happening neurally:** Interrupting the narrative self-model in real time — the DMN's autobiographical simulation — by catching its products mid-arising. Phenomenological studies of boundary dissolution list precisely this "suspension of the first-person perspective" as a deepening marker (Trautwein et al., 2024; Nave et al.). (Interpretive — the mapping is the site's, not a study's claim.)

## 4. The vividness experiment

**Do:** Look at any object for 30 seconds. First, notice the *labels* the mind pastes on it ("cup, white, mine"). Then deliberately drop the labels and meet the raw visual texture.

**What to notice:** The jump in vividness when labels fall away — the "more real than real" of Card 5 in miniature.

**What may be happening neurally:** Dropping conceptual labels = relaxing high-level predictive priors, letting sensory data arrive with higher precision — the REBUS mechanism (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019). The Open Presence EEG pattern (Fucci et al., 2018) is the lab version of this. (Plausible mapping, not a direct finding.)

## 5. Finding your place (Dōgen's version)

**Do:** Dōgen's *Genjōkōan*: *"When you find your place where you are, practice occurs, actualizing the fundamental point… for the place, the way, is neither large nor small, neither yours nor others."* Sit exactly where you are — not getting settled, not improving the posture, not waiting for calm — and let *this place, as it is*, be the whole practice.

**What to notice:** The subtle background project of being somewhere *else* (a better seat, a calmer mind, a later time) — and what happens when it's dropped: the place stops being a location and becomes the practice itself.

**What may be happening neurally:** Ceasing spatial self-projection quiets the parietal self-location machinery (cf. Newberg et al., 2001; Dor-Ziderman et al., 2016). (Interpretive.)

## Safety and honesty notes (site must include)

- These are pointers, not techniques and not therapy. People with trauma histories or active mental-health conditions should treat intense meditation the way they'd treat intense exercise — with guidance.
- Angelo's own warning belongs here: *"Don't ever compare your own experience to anything I've said or anyone said. Your immediate experience is the best thing there to wake you up — just as it is."*
- No pointer reliably produces awakening — *"you can't control when an awakening happens."* The site sells nothing.
