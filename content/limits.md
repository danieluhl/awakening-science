# Honest limits — what the science doesn't show

This page should be prominent on the site and linked from every science panel. It's the credibility engine.

## 1. Correlation, not causation

Nearly every finding is correlational: meditators' brains differ from controls', or a brain state co-occurs with a reported experience. Nothing here proves a brain event *causes* non-dual awareness — or the reverse. The site must never say "this brain region produces awakening."

## 2. The hard problem is untouched

No study explains *why* integrated posterior activity, beta suppression, or relaxed priors should feel like anything at all. The explanatory gap between neural correlates and first-person experience is fully open (Morrow, Reinerman-Jones & Gallagher, 2013). The site maps correlates, not causes.

## 3. Tiny samples of rare people

The sharpest findings come from single cases or small expert cohorts: one adept at 7T (Yang et al., 2025), one 20,000-hour meditator in MEG (Dor-Ziderman et al., 2016), 22 meditators (Linares Gutiérrez et al., 2022), 33 TM practitioners (Canales-Johnson et al., 2026), 34 experts (Reggente et al., 2025). Experts differ from the rest of us in unknown ways — selection effects, not just training effects.

## 4. Demand characteristics

Meditators know what the study is about. The neurofeedback finding on "effortless awareness" (Garrison et al., 2013) comes with the authors' own flag: one participant revised her phenomenological report to match the feedback graph. Experienced meditators are also often *committed* to the frameworks being tested.

## 5. The reportability paradox

Studying "contentless" or "non-dual" states with questionnaires and button-presses is inherently paradoxical: the instrument (a reporting self) is what the state dissolves. Trautwein et al. (2024) found beta-suppression correlated with *interview-derived* change but **not** with classical self-report scales — the standard instruments miss the phenomenon.

## 6. Construct validity is unsettled

It's an open question whether "non-dual awareness," "pure consciousness," "minimal phenomenal experience," and "cessation" are distinct, valid constructs — "not yet enough data" (Millière et al., 2018). Studies use the terms loosely; the site should keep them distinct where the literature does (e.g. Laukkonen et al., 2023: cessation ≠ pure consciousness ≠ non-dual awareness).

## 7. The psychedelic analogy is partial

Psychedelic ego-dissolution is usually content-flooded (vivid hallucinations); meditative non-duality is sparse. Meditation involves attentional control and agency the drug state lacks. "Same machinery" is the honest framing; "same state" is not (Millière et al., 2018).

## 8. No lab has captured the "click"

Awakening-as-recognition (Card 8) is sudden, rare, and unplannable — the worst possible laboratory target. The site presents the phenomenology precisely and the neuroscience as "still catching up." That's a feature, not a gap to paper over.

## 9. Verification tiers

Some findings were verified from abstracts or press reports rather than full text (flagged in SOURCES.md: Canales-Johnson 2026, Fucci 2018, Laukkonen & Slagter 2021, Brown 2018, Reggente 2025, Ogishima 2023, Metzinger 2024, Newberg 2001). The site's bibliography should prefer canonical journal links and drop or downgrade any claim that can't be traced to a primary source.

## The bottom line for the site

Everything on the science side is *consistent with* and *suggestive of* the first-person reports — nothing *proves* them, and nothing reduces them. The honest sentence, repeated where needed: **"The brain data and the first-person reports are pointing at the same territory from opposite sides. Neither side owns it."**
